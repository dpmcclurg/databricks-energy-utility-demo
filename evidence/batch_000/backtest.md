# Backtest on the synthetic regions: Excel-style baseline against the new model

Run: batch `000`, as of `2026-11-04`. Generated by `src/notebooks/08_evidence.py` from the tables.

## Method

Held-out winters 2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26. Each model is trained only on the five gas years before the winter. `mape_*` = monthly mean absolute percentage error, Nov-Mar. Baseline and model are compared twice: given the actual weather (`*_actual_weather`, the method's own error) and forecasting with normal weather (`mape_model`, `mape_baseline`: what a buyer knows in October). The regional volumes are synthetic, so this checks the code; `state_backtest.md` checks the method on real data.

## Reading the results

**Given the weather that happened**, which isolates the method's own error, the new model's monthly error is 1.9% against 4.4% for the baseline, averaged over the held-out winters.

**Forecasting in October with normal weather**, both errors are mostly the weather of that winter. 3 of 3 held-out winters were warmer than normal (table below). The baseline fits the last five years of volume, so it plans on that window's average customer count, about 3.4% fewer customers than each winter had. In a warm winter that low bias offsets the weather miss, so the baseline comes out closer; in a cold winter the two errors add up and it under-buys. The new model plans on the customers it expects. The two weather normals (every earlier winter for the model, the last 10 for the baseline) differ by about 1%, so they are not the cause.

Months inside the P10 to P90 range, reported as-is: 2023-24 84.0%, 2024-25 44.0%, 2025-26 16.0%. Three winters is a small sample. Which weather normal to plan on (every earlier winter, a recent window, or a trended normal) is a planning assumption FP&A sets, like the data rules.
